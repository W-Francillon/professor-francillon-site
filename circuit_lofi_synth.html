1. Why Lo-Fi Sounds “Warm” (1st-Order RC)

Raw sawtooth and square waves contain rich high-frequency Fourier harmonics. Increasing R or C lowers the cutoff frequency f_c = \dfrac{1}{2\pi RC}, attenuating those upper harmonics at -20\text{ dB/decade} and rounding the oscilloscope waveform into mellow café warmth.

2. Sculpting Rain with a 2nd-Order RLC Tank

Pink noise contains continuous energy across the audio band. Passing it through a series RLC bandpass filter centers the rain texture at f_0 = \dfrac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}. Lowering R raises the Quality Factor Q = \dfrac{1}{R}\sqrt{\dfrac{L}{C}}, turning soft rain into a resonant wind-tunnel whistle.

3. Try This Experiment

Hit Play Chords + Chill Beat, switch the oscillator between SAW, SQR, and TRI, and slowly drag Stage 1 R from 1\text{ k}\Omega up to 80\text{ k}\Omega. Watch the cyan f_c line sweep left across the pink FFT spectrum while tapping keys Z, X, and C for deep 808 bass.

Breadboard-Ready Hardware Schematics (3.5mm Aux Input \to Filter \to LM386 Speaker Amp)

To build these exact filters on a physical breadboard, each passive stage feeds into an LM386N-1 single-supply audio power amplifier (powered by a single +9\text{ V} battery—no -9\text{ V} rail is needed). The LM386 provides a high input impedance (50\text{ k}\Omega at Pin 3) so it does not load down the filter, while internally biasing Pin 5 to +4.5\text{ V} DC and driving an 8\text{ }\Omega speaker through a 220\text{ }\mu\text{F} DC-blocking capacitor.

STAGE 1: 1ST-ORDER RC LOW-PASS + LM386 AMP Chord Warmth Build
  • Required Input: Connect a phone or laptop via a 3.5mm TRS breakout playing a synth track or raw square/sawtooth tone.
  • How It Behaves: Turning the 50\text{ k}\Omega potentiometer sweeps f_c = \frac{1}{2\pi RC} down into the hundreds of Hertz, shunting upper harmonics through the 10\text{ nF} capacitor to ground.
  • Why the 220\text{ }\mu\text{F} Cap Matters: Pin 5 sits at +4.5\text{ V} DC. The 220\text{ }\mu\text{F} electrolytic capacitor (+ lead toward Pin 5) blocks DC while passing the AC audio signal to the 8\text{ }\Omega speaker.
STAGE 2: SERIES RLC BANDPASS + LM386 AMP Café Rain Build
  • Required Input: Feed broadband white or pink noise from a phone/laptop (or benchtop function generator) into the 3.5mm jack.
  • How It Behaves: At f_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{(100\text{ mH})(220\text{ nF})}} \approx 1.07\text{ kHz}, the reactances +jX_L and -jX_C cancel out, letting the rain band pass across the 1\text{ k}\Omega potentiometer into Pin 3 of the LM386.
  • Tuning the Rain: Sweeping the 1\text{ k}\Omega pot adjusts Q = \frac{1}{R}\sqrt{\frac{L}{C}} live—turning wide café rain into a narrow resonant whistle.

LM386N-1 Physical Pinout (8-Pin DIP) & Datasheet Reference

Breadboard Wiring & Sanity Check:

  • Chip Orientation: Place the LM386 straddling the center trench of your breadboard with the half-circle notch facing up (Pin 1 is top-left, Pin 8 is top-right).
  • Common Ground: Connect the 9\text{ V} battery negative (-), the 3.5mm jack ground (GND), Pin 2, Pin 4, and the speaker’s negative terminal all to the same blue ground rail.
  • Power Decoupling: Place the 100\text{ }\mu\text{F} electrolytic capacitor directly across Pin 6 (+9\text{ V}) and Pin 4 (GND) (+ lead to Pin 6) to stop battery “motorboating” hum.
  • Optional 200\times Gain Mod: Leave Pins 1 and 8 open for 20\text{ V/V} (26\text{ dB}) gain, or bridge them with a 10\text{ }\mu\text{F} capacitor for 200\text{ V/V} (46\text{ dB}) punchy bass.
