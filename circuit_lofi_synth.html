1. Why Lo-Fi Sounds “Warm” (1st-Order RC)

Raw triangle and square waves contain sharp high-frequency harmonics. Increasing R or C lowers the cutoff frequency f_c = \dfrac{1}{2\pi RC}, attenuating those upper harmonics at -20\text{ dB/decade} and rounding the oscilloscope waveform into mellow café warmth.

2. Sculpting Rain with a 2nd-Order RLC Tank

White noise contains equal energy at all frequencies. Passing it through a series RLC bandpass filter centers the rain texture at f_0 = \dfrac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}. Lowering R raises the Quality Factor Q = \dfrac{1}{R}\sqrt{\dfrac{L}{C}}, turning soft rain into a resonant wind-tunnel whistle.

3. Try This Experiment

Hit Play Lo-Fi Loop, turn on Café Rain, and slowly drag the Stage 1 capacitor C from 5\text{ nF} up to 80\text{ nF}. Watch the cyan cutoff line sweep left across the pink FFT spectrum as the high-frequency sparkle vanishes.

Real-World Analog Hardware Schematics (Input \to Filter \to Speaker)

To build these exact audio stages on a physical breadboard, each passive filter must be isolated from the low-impedance speaker (8\text{ }\Omega) using a unity-gain op-amp buffer so the speaker does not load down the filter and shift f_c or f_0.

STAGE 1: 1ST-ORDER RC LOW-PASS FILTER Chord Warmth Stage
  • Required Input: Polyphonic audio waveform v_{\text{in}}(t) (triangle + sine chord oscillator, \approx 130\text{–}330\text{ Hz} fundamentals plus upper harmonics).
  • How It Behaves: At low frequencies, X_C = \frac{1}{2\pi f C} is very large (open circuit), so the fundamental chord notes pass cleanly to the speaker. Above f_c = \frac{1}{2\pi RC}, X_C drops and shunts harsh high-frequency harmonics directly to ground.
  • Speaker Interface: The unity-gain op-amp buffer provides high input impedance (Z_{\text{in}} \to \infty) so the 8\text{ }\Omega speaker voice coil does not draw current from the RC node.
STAGE 2: 2ND-ORDER SERIES RLC BANDPASS Café Rain Stage
  • Required Input: Broadband pink/white noise source v_{\text{noise}}(t) containing continuous energy across the entire audible spectrum (20\text{ Hz} \to 20\text{ kHz}).
  • How It Behaves: At resonance (f_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}), inductive reactance +jX_L and capacitive reactance -jX_C cancel out (Z_{LC} = 0), allowing maximum voltage across R to reach the speaker. Frequencies far above or below f_0 are blocked by L or C.
  • Tuning the Rain: Lowering R narrows the passband bandwidth (BW = f_0 / Q), shifting the audio from wide, soft rain into a sharp resonant whistle.
