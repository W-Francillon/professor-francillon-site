1. Why Lo-Fi Sounds "Warm" (1st-Order RC)

Raw triangle and square waves contain sharp high-frequency harmonics. Increasing R or C lowers the cutoff frequency fc = 1 / (2πRC), attenuating those upper harmonics at −20 dB/decade and rounding the oscilloscope waveform into mellow café warmth.

2. Sculpting Rain with a 2nd-Order RLC Tank

White noise contains equal energy at all frequencies. Passing it through a series RLC bandpass filter centers the rain texture at f0 = 1 / (2π√(LC)). Lowering R raises the Quality Factor Q = (1/R)√(L/C), turning soft rain into a resonant wind-tunnel whistle.

3. Try This Experiment

Hit Play Lo-Fi Loop, turn on Café Rain, and slowly drag the Stage 1 capacitor C from 5 nF up to 80 nF. Watch the cyan cutoff line sweep left across the pink FFT spectrum as the high-frequency sparkle vanishes.
