Rigid-Body Planar Kinematics & the Instantaneous Center of Zero Velocity

From pure translation and fixed-axis rotation to general planar motion, any rigid body moving in a plane can be modeled at a given instant as rotating purely about a unique stationary pivot called the Instantaneous Center of Zero Velocity (ICZV). Explore the mathematical proofs, test four classic engineering mechanisms in real time, and verify your mastery with interactive problem challenges.

1. Classification of Rigid-Body Planar Motion

A rigid body is an idealized solid in which the distance between any two points remains strictly constant (\|\mathbf{r}_{B/A}\| = \text{constant}) regardless of the forces or moments applied. Planar (2D) rigid-body motion is classified into three foundational regimes:

Regime 1 • Zero Rotation (\omega = 0)

Pure Translation

Every line segment on the body remains parallel to its original orientation. Because \mathbf{r}_{B/A} is constant in both magnitude and direction, \dfrac{d\mathbf{r}_{B/A}}{dt} = \mathbf{0}, so every point shares the exact same velocity and acceleration: \mathbf{v}_B = \mathbf{v}_A, \qquad \mathbf{a}_B = \mathbf{a}_A

Regime 2 • Fixed Pivot (O)

Pure Rotation About a Fixed Axis

Every point P at radial distance r from fixed pin O sweeps a circular arc s = r\theta. Linear speed scales with radius (v = \omega r), while acceleration splits into tangential (speed change) and normal (centripetal) components: a_t = \alpha r, \qquad a_n = \omega^2 r = \frac{v^2}{r}

Regime 3 • Translation + Rotation

General Planar Motion

The body simultaneously translates and rotates (such as a connecting rod, sliding ladder, or rolling wheel). We analyze velocity via Vector Superposition (\mathbf{v}_B = \mathbf{v}_A + \boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{r}_{B/A}) or via the Instantaneous Center (IC).

💡 Thought Experiment: Sliding a Box Across a Lab Bench (Pure Translation)

Imagine a heavy wooden box resting on a flat, horizontal lab bench. You mark Point A at the top-left corner and Point B at the bottom-right corner, then push the box straight across the table without tipping.

Because the box is rigid and does not rotate, the relative position vector \mathbf{r}_{B/A} has constant magnitude and constant angle. Taking the time derivative of \mathbf{r}_B = \mathbf{r}_A + \mathbf{r}_{B/A} yields \mathbf{v}_B = \mathbf{v}_A + \mathbf{0} and \mathbf{a}_B = \mathbf{a}_A. Every point on a translating body shares the exact same kinematics!

2. General Planar Motion: Algebraic Vector Method vs. Geometric ICZV Method

By Chasles’ Theorem, any rigid body undergoing general planar motion can be treated at a specific microsecond as rotating purely about an invisible, temporary stationary point called the Instantaneous Center of Zero Velocity (IC or ICZV) where \mathbf{v}_{IC} = \mathbf{0}.

Feature Method 1: Relative Velocity Equation (Algebraic / Vector) Method 2: Instantaneous Center of Zero Velocity (Geometric / ICZV)
Core Principle Superposition of translation of reference point A plus pure rotation of B relative to A. Pure rotation of the entire rigid body about a temporary stationary pivot point IC where vIC = 0.
Governing Equation vB = vA + (ω × rB/A) vA = ω · rA/IC  and  vB = ω · rB/IC  (Scalar)
Primary Strengths Systematic, purely algebraic, extends directly to 3D kinematics and relative acceleration equations. Fast, visual, bypasses cross-product sign errors, and reveals velocity distributions across linkages immediately.
Key Limitations Cross products with i, j, k unit vectors require careful bookkeeping of signs and quadrant angles. Strictly valid for velocity only! Because aIC ≠ 0 in general, IC cannot be used for accelerations.

Four Geometric Rules for Locating the Instantaneous Center (IC)

Case 1 • Non-Parallel Velocity Directions

Intersection of Perpendiculars

Because every point moves tangent to a circle centered at IC, the radius vector \mathbf{r}_{P/IC} is always perpendicular to \mathbf{v}_P. Draw a line \perp to \mathbf{v}_A through A and a line \perp to \mathbf{v}_B through B; their intersection is IC.

Case 2 • Known Velocity \mathbf{v}_A & Angular Speed \omega

Radial Distance Along the Normal

If you know the velocity vector \mathbf{v}_A of a single point and the body’s angular velocity \omega, construct a line perpendicular to \mathbf{v}_A at A and measure distance r_{A/IC} = \dfrac{v_A}{\omega} toward the side that produces the correct rotational sense.

Case 3 • No-Slip Contact (Wheels, Gears, Cords)

Point of Tangency is IC

When a rigid wheel rolls without slipping on a stationary ground (or unwinds from a taut, stationary vertical cord), the point of contact C (or tangency E) has zero instantaneous velocity (\mathbf{v}_{IC} = \mathbf{0}).

Case 4 • Parallel Velocities (\mathbf{v}_A \parallel \mathbf{v}_B)

Similar Triangles Construction

When \mathbf{v}_A and \mathbf{v}_B are parallel and perpendicular to line AB, IC lies on line AB at the intersection with the line joining the tips of \mathbf{v}_A and \mathbf{v}_B: \omega = \dfrac{|v_A - v_B|}{d_{AB}} (same sense) or \omega = \dfrac{v_A + v_B}{d_{AB}} (opposite sense).

Problem 1A: Variable Angular Velocity

The angular velocity of a disk of radius r = 0.8\text{ m} is defined by \omega(t) = (5t^2 + 2)\text{ rad/s}, where t is in seconds. Determine the magnitudes of the velocity and total acceleration of point A on the outer edge of the disk when t = 0.5\text{ s}.

  1. Evaluate \omega and \alpha = \dfrac{d\omega}{dt} at t = 0.5\text{ s}: \omega(0.5) = 5(0.5)^2 + 2 = \mathbf{3.25\text{ rad/s}}, \qquad \alpha(0.5) = \frac{d}{dt}(5t^2+2)\Big|_{t=0.5} = 10(0.5) = \mathbf{5.00\text{ rad/s}^2}
  2. Linear Speed and Orthogonal Acceleration Components: v_A = \omega r = (3.25)(0.8) = \mathbf{2.60\text{ m/s}} a_t = \alpha r = (5.00)(0.8) = 4.00\text{ m/s}^2, \qquad a_n = \omega^2 r = (3.25)^2(0.8) = 8.45\text{ m/s}^2 a_A = \sqrt{a_t^2 + a_n^2} = \sqrt{(4.00)^2 + (8.45)^2} = \mathbf{9.35\text{ m/s}^2}

Problem 1B: Constant Angular Acceleration (Points A & B)

A disk is originally rotating at \omega_0 = 8\text{ rad/s} and is subjected to a constant angular acceleration \alpha = 6\text{ rad/s}^2:

  • Part (i): Find the velocity and normal/tangential acceleration components of outer point A (r_A = 2\text{ ft}) at t = 0.5\text{ s}.
  • Part (ii): Find the velocity and acceleration components of inner point B (r_B = 1.5\text{ ft}) just after the wheel undergoes 2 complete revolutions (\Delta\theta = 4\pi\text{ rad}).

  • Part (i) Point A at t = 0.5\text{ s}: \begin{aligned} \omega &= \omega_0 + \alpha t = 8 + 6(0.5) = \mathbf{11.0\text{ rad/s}} \\ v_A &= \omega r_A = (11)(2) = \mathbf{22.0\text{ ft/s}} \\ (a_A)_t &= \alpha r_A = (6)(2) = \mathbf{12.0\text{ ft/s}^2}, \qquad (a_A)_n = \omega^2 r_A = (11)^2(2) = \mathbf{242\text{ ft/s}^2} \end{aligned}

  • Part (ii) Point B after \Delta\theta = 2\text{ rev} = 4\pi\text{ rad}: \begin{aligned} \omega^2 &= \omega_0^2 + 2\alpha\Delta\theta = 8^2 + 2(6)(4\pi) = 214.80 \implies \omega = \mathbf{14.66\text{ rad/s}} \\ v_B &= \omega r_B = (14.66)(1.5) = \mathbf{22.0\text{ ft/s}} \\ (a_B)_t &= \alpha r_B = \mathbf{9.00\text{ ft/s}^2}, \qquad (a_B)_n = \omega^2 r_B = \mathbf{322\text{ ft/s}^2} \end{aligned}

Problem Statement

A L = 10\text{ ft} ladder rests against a vertical wall. The top of the ladder (Point A) slides straight down the wall at a constant velocity of v_A = 3\text{ ft/s}. At the instant shown, the ladder makes a \theta = 60^\circ angle with the horizontal floor. Determine the velocity v_B of the bottom of the ladder and the angular velocity \omega using both methods.

Method A: Instantaneous Center (Geometric)

  1. Locate ICZV: Draw a horizontal line \perp to \mathbf{v}_A from A and a vertical line \perp to \mathbf{v}_B from B.
  2. Radial Distances from ICZV: r_{A/IC} = 10\cos(60^\circ) = 5.00\text{ ft}, \qquad r_{B/IC} = 10\sin(60^\circ) = 8.66\text{ ft}
  3. Solve for \omega and v_B: \omega = \frac{v_A}{r_{A/IC}} = \frac{3\text{ ft/s}}{5.00\text{ ft}} = \mathbf{0.60\text{ rad/s}\ (\text{CCW})}, \qquad v_B = \omega r_{B/IC} = (0.60)(8.66) = \mathbf{5.20\text{ ft/s}\ (\rightarrow)}

Method B: Relative Velocity Equation (Vector Algebra)

  1. Express Vectors: \mathbf{v}_A = -3\hat{\mathbf{j}}\text{ ft/s}, \mathbf{v}_B = v_B\hat{\mathbf{i}}, \boldsymbol{\omega} = \omega\hat{\mathbf{k}}, and \mathbf{r}_{B/A} = 5\hat{\mathbf{i}} - 8.66\hat{\mathbf{j}}\text{ ft}.
  2. Substitute into \mathbf{v}_B = \mathbf{v}_A + \boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r}_{B/A}: v_B\hat{\mathbf{i}} = -3\hat{\mathbf{j}} + (\omega\hat{\mathbf{k}}) \times (5\hat{\mathbf{i}} - 8.66\hat{\mathbf{j}}) = 8.66\omega\hat{\mathbf{i}} + (5\omega - 3)\hat{\mathbf{j}}
  3. Equate Components: 5\omega - 3 = 0 \implies \mathbf{\omega = 0.60\text{ rad/s}} and v_B = 8.66(0.60) = \mathbf{5.20\text{ ft/s}}.

Problem Statement

In an internal combustion engine, crank OA of length r = 0.15\text{ m} rotates counter-clockwise at \omega_{OA} = 10\text{ rad/s} at the instant \theta = 45^\circ. Connecting rod AB has length L = 0.45\text{ m}, and piston B is constrained to move horizontally along the x-axis. Determine the angular velocity \omega_{AB} of the connecting rod and the horizontal velocity v_B of the piston.

  1. Crank Pin Velocity at A: v_A = \omega_{OA} r = (10\text{ rad/s})(0.15\text{ m}) = \mathbf{1.50\text{ m/s}} \quad (\perp \text{to crank } OA)
  2. Connecting Rod Angle \beta (Law of Sines): \sin\beta = \frac{r\sin(45^\circ)}{L} = \frac{0.15(0.7071)}{0.45} = 0.2357 \implies \beta = 13.63^\circ, \quad \cos\beta = 0.9718
  3. Locate IC of Connecting Rod AB:
    • The line \perp to \mathbf{v}_A is the extension of crank OA (at 45^\circ).
    • The line \perp to horizontal piston velocity \mathbf{v}_B is a vertical line passing through B at horizontal coordinate x_B = r\cos(45^\circ) + L\cos\beta = 0.1061 + 0.4373 = 0.5434\text{ m}.
    • Because the OA line has slope \tan(45^\circ) = 1, the vertical coordinate of IC is y_{IC} = x_B \tan(45^\circ) = 0.5434\text{ m}.
  4. Distances from IC and Final Velocities: r_{B/IC} = y_{IC} = 0.5434\text{ m}, \qquad r_{A/IC} = \sqrt{(0.5434 - 0.1061)^2 + (0.5434 - 0.1061)^2} = 0.6184\text{ m} \omega_{AB} = \frac{v_A}{r_{A/IC}} = \frac{1.50}{0.6184} = \mathbf{2.43\text{ rad/s}\ (\text{CW})}, \qquad v_B = \omega_{AB} r_{B/IC} = (2.425)(0.5434) = \mathbf{1.32\text{ m/s}\ (\leftarrow)}

CHALLENGE 1: THE UNWINDING DISK No-Slip Cord Tangency

Problem 1: A disk of radius r = 6\text{ in.} (0.5\text{ ft}) unwinds from an inextensible vertical rope fixed at D with angular speed \omega_d = 20\text{ rad/s} (CCW). Determine the speeds (in \text{ft/s}) of points B (left rim) and A (top rim).

Show Symbolic & Numerical Solution
Because rope DE is stationary, tangency point E is the ICZV (vE = 0):
• Center O: vO = −ωdr j = −(20)(0.5)j = −10 ft/s j
• Point B: Distance from E is 2r → vB = −2ωdr j = −20.0 ft/s j (|vB| = 20 ft/s)
• Point A: rA/E = −ri + rj → vA = −ωdr i − ωdr j = −10i − 10j ft/s (|vA| = 10√2 = 14.14 ft/s)
• Point C: vC = +10i − 10j ft/s (|vC| = 14.14 ft/s)
CHALLENGE 2: THE RUSTY GATE Sliding Contact Constraint

Problem 2: A heavy gate hinged at O is pushed closed by car bumper A at a constant horizontal distance w = 24\text{ m} from O. If the car moves straight downward (\downarrow) at v_A = 1.2\text{ m/s}, find the angular speed \omega (\text{rad/s}) of the gate when \theta = 15^\circ.

Show Geometric & Calculus Solution
The car contact point stays at constant horizontal distance w = 24 m, so its vertical position is yA = w tan θ.
Differentiating with respect to time:
˙yA = w sec2(θ) · ˙θ &implies; ω = (vA cos2θ) / w
At θ = 15°: ω = 1.2 × cos2(15°) / 24 = (1.2 × 0.9330) / 24 = 0.0467 rad/s (CW).
CHALLENGE 3: WHEEL & SLIDER BAR Orthogonal ICZV

Problem 3: A wheel (R = 14\text{ in.}) rolls right with center speed v_B = 7\text{ ft/s} (84\text{ in./s}). Bar AB (L = 40\text{ in.}) connects center B to vertical slider A. When \theta = 72^\circ, find \omega_{AB} (\text{rad/s}) and slider speed v_A (\text{ft/s}).

Show ICZV & Vector Solution
Slider A moves vertically (⊥ ray is horizontal) and wheel center B moves horizontally (⊥ ray is vertical).
• rB/IC = L sin(72°) = 40(0.9511) = 38.04 in. = 3.170 ft
• rA/IC = L cos(72°) = 40(0.3090) = 12.36 in. = 1.030 ft
• ωAB = vB / rB/IC = 7 ft/s / 3.170 ft = 2.21 rad/s (CCW)
• vA = ωAB · rA/IC = (2.208)(1.030) = 2.27 ft/s (↓)
